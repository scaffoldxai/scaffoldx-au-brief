# AU Brief · 28 June 2026

## 1. CULTURE & GENERAL

**Socceroos through to the knockouts.** Australia finished second in Group D at the 2026 FIFA World Cup after a 0–0 draw with Paraguay on 26 June — surviving a nervy group stage that included a 2–0 win over Türkiye and a 2–0 loss to the USA. They face a Group G qualifier on 4 July in Arlington, Texas. National mood is cautiously optimistic; a safe conversation starter with any client this week.

**Social media fines set to increase.** The federal government is pushing for stiffer penalties against platforms breaching Australia's under-16 social media ban. Compliance obligations and digital policy are moving up the boardroom agenda for consumer-facing businesses.

**Senate greenwashing report lands.** The final report of the Senate inquiry into corporate greenwashing was due 25 June, amplifying ESG reporting pressure on listed companies and large private operators heading into the new financial year.

## 2. BUSINESS & MARKET SIGNALS

**AI agents stuck in pilot purgatory.** McKinsey data shows only 23% of Australian organisations have scaled AI agents across a business function, despite 39% experimenting. The operationalisation gap is the defining challenge — and cost — of 2026.

**M&A activity up 42% year-on-year.** Australian deal-making is running hot: $1.77B raised across 82 equity rounds and 76 acquisitions completed to June. Technology, energy, and real estate lead sectors. Post-transaction system fragmentation reliably follows.

**AUSTRAC compliance clock ticking.** Newly regulated entities and VASPs have until 29 July 2026 to notify AUSTRAC of their AML/CTF compliance officer. The reform wave is forcing financial services firms to automate regulatory reporting and transaction monitoring at pace.

**Construction majors hiring digital systems roles.** Boral is mid-way through a multi-year data infrastructure transformation; Seymour Whyte is recruiting for Senior Technical Officer (Digital Systems). Both are reliable proxies for project-level technology investment in a sector under sustained margin pressure.

**Digital skills gap accelerating outsourcing demand.** AI and automation skill demand has surged 245% since 2023, against a projected shortfall of 370,000 digitally skilled workers. Organisations are increasingly turning to external partners to build and run automated workflows rather than hiring in-house.
